Ocarina of Time · hum to play · 20 min talk + 10 min live demo

Hum a song, and the computer names it

How the ocarina tool turns a few seconds of humming into one of 12 song names. There's no machine learning and no training data, just dot products, a change of basis, a matrix factorization and a distance.

You need basic linear algebra. Everything about sound, we'll build from scratch.

Diivanand Ramalingam

The problem

In: 44,100 numbers a second. Out: one of 12 names.

Signal
A quantity that changes over time. Here it's air pressure at the microphone.
Sample
One measurement of the signal: a single float.
Sample rate \(f_s\)
Samples per second. Ours is 44,100, so three seconds of humming is an array of about 130,000 floats, and that's all we get.
  • No entry says "D4". The pitch is in how the numbers repeat, so we have to compute it.
  • People hum low, high, sharp or flat, so the answer must not depend on key.

Sound · from scratch

Pitch is how often the waveform repeats

Period \(T\)
How long a repeating pattern takes to repeat once.
Frequency \(f\)
Repeats per second, \(f = 1/T\), measured in hertz (Hz). For a sinusoid, its cycles per second.
Pitch
How high or low a sound is heard. For a sound that repeats, the repetition rate sets it: the fundamental frequency \(f_0 = 1/T\). In this talk, "pitch" means \(f_0\) in Hz.
Harmonics
Sinusoids at \(f_0, 2f_0, 3f_0, \ldots\) that add up to a voice. They shape its tone (timbre), not its pitch, because the sum still repeats every \(1/f_0\).
Note
A named pitch on the 12-tone scale: A4 = 440 Hz, D4 = 293.7 Hz. Neighbouring notes are a semitone apart, a factor of \(2^{1/12} \approx 1.059\). Twelve semitones double \(f\): an octave.

So pitch is multiplicative: equal musical steps are equal ratios, not equal numbers of Hz.

\[ x(t) = \sum_{h \ge 1} a_h \sin\bigl(2\pi\, h f_0\, t + \varphi_h\bigr) \]

Sound · two descriptions

Time domain and frequency domain: one signal, two coordinate systems

Time domain
The signal as its value at each instant: \(x(t)\), or samples \(x_0, \ldots, x_{N-1}\). In coordinates, it's the standard basis: one basis vector per instant.
Sinusoid
\(a\cos(2\pi f t + \varphi)\): a frequency f, an amplitude a (how big) and a phase φ (where its peaks sit in time).
Frequency domain
The same signal as a recipe of sinusoids: an amplitude and a phase for each frequency. The amplitudes plotted against frequency are the spectrum.
Fourier transform
The change of coordinates from the time domain to the frequency domain. It's invertible, so nothing is lost. The DFT is the version for N samples (slides 6–7).
  • Pitch is hard to read off a wiggly line but easy to read off the spectrum: a row of peaks at \(f_0, 2f_0, 3f_0\).
  • Drag the phases. The waveform changes shape, but the amplitudes and the pitch don't. So the recognizer keeps only the magnitudes \(|X|\) and throws the phases away.

Frames · stage 1

Cut the recording into 40 ms frames. Each one is a vector.

  • The pitch changes over the song but holds for tens of milliseconds. So every 10 ms (the hop) we take the next 40 ms (a frame). Frames overlap, and there are 100 a second.
  • At \(f_s = 44{,}100\) a frame has \(N = 1{,}764\) samples: a vector \(x \in \mathbb{R}^{1764}\). 40 ms holds 3+ periods of our lowest pitch, 80 Hz.
  • Every step until slide 14 is a function of one frame vector. The question for each one: what is its \(f_0\)?

Spectrum · the key idea

How much of frequency f is in x? Take a dot product.

  • \(\langle x, p\rangle = \sum_n x_n p_n\) is large when x and p line up and near 0 when the products cancel. So build a probe: a sinusoid at f, sampled at the same N instants.
  • If x contains f, the probe stays in step and the products keep one sign. Otherwise they alternate and cancel.
  • A cosine probe misses a sine shifted by a quarter period, so also use a sine probe and take the length of the pair. Complex numbers do that bookkeeping: \(e^{-i\theta} = \cos\theta - i\sin\theta\).
\[ \text{score}(f) = \Bigl|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x_n\, e^{-2\pi i f n / f_s}\Bigr| = \sqrt{\langle x, \cos_f\rangle^2 + \langle x, \sin_f\rangle^2} \]

Spectrum · linear algebra

All probes at once: the DFT matrix, whose rows are orthogonal

  • Put the probes on a grid: row k of F oscillates k times across the frame, at frequency \(k f_s/N\). Then all N scores are one product, \(X = Fx\): the discrete Fourier transform (DFT). Each entry \(X_k\) is a frequency bin.
  • The rows are orthogonal. For \(j \ne k\) the terms of \(\langle f_j, f_k\rangle\) are powers of \(r = e^{2\pi i (k-j)/N} \ne 1\), a root of unity (\(r^N = 1\)). They walk around a closed polygon and the geometric series sums to 0. Drag j and k.
  • So \(F^{*}F = N\,I\). F is unitary up to a factor of N, and \(F/\sqrt N\) is a rotation of \(\mathbb{C}^N\): the inverse is \(F^{*}/N\), and length is kept, \(\lVert x\rVert^2 = \tfrac1N\lVert X\rVert^2\) (Parseval).
\[ F_{kn} = e^{-2\pi i\,kn/N}, \qquad \langle f_j, f_k\rangle = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} r^{n} = \frac{1-r^{N}}{1-r} = 0 \]

Spectrum · the DFT's hidden assumption

The DFT sees a loop: periodic in time and in frequency

  • Every row of F repeats every N samples: \(e^{-2\pi i k(n+N)/N} = e^{-2\pi i kn/N}\). So anything built from the rows, including x rebuilt from X, repeats too. The DFT treats your N samples as one period of an endless loop.
  • That's always possible: tile any finite list end to end and you get a periodic signal, its periodic extension. Nothing is lost. But if the end doesn't flow into the start, the loop has a jump the sound never had, and that jump causes leakage (slide 11).
  • The same holds in frequency: \(F_{k+N,\,n} = F_{kn}\), so \(X_{k+N} = X_k\). Frequencies k and k+N give identical samples (aliasing), so bins \(N/2 \ldots N-1\) are the same as \(-N/2 \ldots -1\): the negative frequencies of slide 10.
\[ x_{n+N} = x_n, \qquad X_{k+N} = X_k \]

Spectrum · why sinusoids?

Fourier vectors diagonalize every shift-invariant matrix

  • Let S shift a vector by one sample, cyclically, since the DFT sees a loop (slide 8): \((Sx)_n = x_{n-1}\). Each Fourier vector is an eigenvector, \(S f_k = e^{-2\pi i k/N} f_k\): shifting a sinusoid only changes its phase.
  • A circulant matrix C, with each row the previous one shifted, is a polynomial in S, so the same basis diagonalizes it. Its eigenvalues are \(Fc\), the DFT of its first column. Circulants are cyclic convolutions, so this is the convolution theorem: \(c \circledast x \;\mapsto\; (Fc) \odot (Fx)\).
  • Payoff 1: a note that starts later in the frame changes only phases, \(|\lambda| = 1\), so \(|X|\), the only thing we keep, stays put. Payoff 2: the dual also holds, so multiplying by a window (a diagonal matrix) convolves the spectrum. That's slide 11.
\[ S = F^{-1} \operatorname{diag}\bigl(e^{-2\pi i k/N}\bigr) F, \qquad C = F^{-1} \operatorname{diag}(Fc)\, F \]

Spectrum · reading NumPy's output

What fft returns: one array, in a particular order

  • Index k is frequency \(k f_s/N\). Bin 0 is the zero frequency (DC): N times the mean, always real. Bins \(1 \ldots N/2-1\) are positive frequencies.
  • Bins past the middle are negative frequencies (slide 8): bin k means \(k-N\), since \(e^{-2\pi i kn/N} = e^{-2\pi i (k-N)n/N}\) on the sample grid. For even N, bin N/2 is the Nyquist frequency \(f_s/2\), the fastest wiggle samples can hold (±1 alternating). Odd N has no such bin. fftfreq labels the bins, and fftshift moves 0 to the middle.
  • Real x gives \(X_{N-k} = \overline{X_k}\): the top half mirrors the bottom, and bins 0 and N/2 are real. So rfft returns only \(\lfloor N/2\rfloor + 1\) bins, for about half the work. A unit cosine at bin k gives \(|X_k| = N/2\), split between +k and −k.

Spectrum · windowing

Notes fall between grid lines: leakage, and the window that fixes it

  • Bin k means exactly k cycles per frame. A hummed note rarely fits a whole number of cycles, so it isn't one basis vector, and its projection spreads over every bin: leakage.
  • The DFT treats the frame as one period of a loop (slide 8), and the loop has a jump at its seam. Fade the frame to 0 at both ends with the Hann window w. By slide 9, \(\operatorname{diag}(w)\) in time is a convolution in frequency, and for Hann it has only 3 taps:
\[ \widehat{w \odot x} = \tfrac1N\, \hat w \circledast \hat x, \qquad \hat w = N\bigl(-\tfrac14,\ \tfrac12,\ -\tfrac14\bigr) \text{ at bins } -1, 0, 1 \]

The trade: the peak widens to ±2 bins, but far bins fall from −13 dB to −31 dB. A decibel is \(20\log_{10}\) of an amplitude ratio, so −20 dB is 10× smaller.

Spectrum · speed

The FFT: the same answer, by divide and conquer

  • \(Fx\) done directly is \(N^2\) multiply-adds. For N = 7,200 at 100 frames a second, that's 5 billion a second.
  • Split x into even and odd samples. Each half needs an N/2-point DFT, and they combine with N/2 "butterflies". Like merge sort: \(T(N) = 2T(N/2) + O(N) = O(N\log N)\). Below, \(\omega = e^{-2\pi i/N}\).
  • As matrices, F factors into \(\log_2 N\) sparse factors with 2 nonzeros per row. SciPy also splits by 3 and 5, so 7,200 = 2⁵·3²·5² is fast. A large prime size would not be.
\[ X_k = E_k + \omega^{k} O_k, \qquad X_{k+N/2} = E_k - \omega^{k} O_k \]

Pitch · stage 3

Which peak is the note? Multiply the evidence.

  • A hum has a peak at every harmonic. The tallest is often \(2f_0\): the octave error from slide 3.
  • The harmonic product spectrum scores each candidate f by \(|X(f)|\,|X(2f)|\,|X(3f)|\). Only the true \(f_0\) has a peak at all three, so it acts like an AND of three tests.
  • A guard: a whistle is nearly a pure sine, so 2f and 3f hold only noise and a subharmonic can win. A candidate must reach 10% of the loudest bin.
\[ P[k] = \prod_{h=1}^{3} \bigl|X[hk]\bigr| \]

Pitch · precision

25 Hz bins can't tell D3 from D♯3: zoom in, then interpolate

  • Bins are \(f_s/N = 44{,}100/1{,}764 = 25\) Hz apart, but at D3 (147 Hz) neighbouring semitones are 8.7 Hz apart.
  • Zero-padding to 7,200 samples (the n=n_fft argument) samples the same smooth curve every 6.1 Hz. The grid is finer, but there's no new information.
  • Then fit a parabola through the top bin and its two neighbours and take the vertex: sub-pixel peak finding. We fit on log magnitude because a windowed peak is close to a Gaussian, whose log is exactly a parabola.
\[ \delta = \frac12\,\frac{\alpha - \gamma}{\alpha - 2\beta + \gamma}, \qquad \hat f = (k + \delta)\,\frac{f_s}{N_{\mathrm{fft}}} \]

Notes · stage 4

From hertz to notes, in the singer's own tuning

  • Semitones multiply, so take a log: \(m = 69 + 12\log_2(f/440)\). A semitone is now +1, and A4 is 69 (the MIDI numbering). Round to get a note.
  • Few people hum in tune with A440. Someone 45 cents sharp (a cent is 1/100 of a semitone) sits near the rounding boundary, and their notes split.
  • The fractional part is circular (0.99 is next to 0.01). Put each frame on the unit circle and average: the angle of the mean is the tuning offset. Subtract it, then round.
  • Then frames become notes. Runs under 80 ms are slides and are dropped, and a repeated note needs a 30 ms gap.
\[ \text{detune} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\arg\Bigl(\frac1T\sum_{t} e^{2\pi i\, m_t}\Bigr) \]

Song · stage 5

Match in any key: a melody is a vector, a key change is a line

  • A song is a vector of note numbers \(s \in \mathbb{Z}^{L}\), with L = 5 to 8. Humming it c semitones lower gives \(s - c\mathbf 1\), so all keys of one song form a line along \(\mathbf 1 = (1, \ldots, 1)\).
  • The error is the L1 distance to that line. The best shift is the median of the differences (in L2 it would be the mean), so one wrong note can't drag the key.
  • Slide each song over every window of your notes, keep the best, and accept if \(E \le 0.5\). The two closest songs are 1.17 apart, so by the triangle inequality no hum is within 0.5 of both.
\[ E(x, s) = \min_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\ \frac1L \bigl\lVert x - s - c\mathbf{1} \bigr\rVert_1, \qquad c^{\star} = \operatorname{median}(x - s) \]

Why not Shazam? A different question needs a different tool

  • Small, fixed and well separated. 12 songs are known in advance, and the closest pair is 1.17 apart against a 0.5 tolerance. Comparing against all of them is instant, and a threshold can safely say "no match".
  • A cooperative player. One sustained note at a time, on purpose, usually in a quiet room: the easy case for tracking \(f_0\).
  • The game only needs which melody. Throwing away timing, timbre and key isn't a loss. It's exactly the invariance we want, bought with a median and a logarithm instead of training data.

The code

One pipeline, six files

What happens when you run ocarina listen:

  1. cli.py · cmd_listen parses the flags and calls audio.record(on_update=LiveView(...)).

  2. audio.py · record streams mic blocks through PortAudio (sounddevice). Every 0.5 s, LiveView re-runs steps 3–4 on the audio so far and stops as soon as a song matches.

  3. recognizer.py · analyze: stages 1–4. normalize → track_pitch [sliding_window_view → magnitude_spectrum → harmonic_product → refine_peaks] → hz_to_midi → estimate_detune → segment_notes → Analysis(times, frequencies, detune, notes)

  4. recognizer.py · rank_songs: stage 5, against songs.OCARINA_SONGS. It returns a SongMatch(name, error, start, transpose) per song, best first.

  5. cli.py prints the report, synth.render + audio.play answer on the ocarina, and plot.save_report draws five panels, reusing magnitude_spectrum and harmonic_product.

recognizer.pyEverything in this talk. DetectorConfig, a frozen dataclass, holds every constant: 40 ms frames, 10 ms hop, ×4 padding, 80–1,200 Hz, 3 harmonics, the 10% floor, 80 ms notes, 30 ms rests.
songs.pyThe five-note scale and the 12 songs as tuples of MIDI numbers, plus note_name and find_song("storms").
synth.pyrender() sums harmonics under an envelope: an ocarina for playback and a hum for tests. write_wav saves it.
audio.pyMic and speakers. sounddevice is imported lazily, so everything else works without PortAudio, and the tests swap in a fake.
plot.pyThe five-panel matplotlib report you'll see in the demo.
cli.pyThe ocarina command (listen, identify, play, songs, synth, devices, slides) and the live meter.
tests/24 song tests: hum each song 9.6 semitones down (40 cents sharp) and it must match; hum it backwards and it must not. Plus CLI tests that run listen against a fake microphone.

Appendix

Glossary

Signal
A quantity that changes over time; here, air pressure at the microphone.
Sample · sample rate \(f_s\)
One measurement of the signal; the number of samples per second (44,100).
Period · frequency · hertz
The time to repeat once, T; repeats per second, f = 1/T, measured in hertz (Hz).
Pitch · fundamental \(f_0\)
How high a sound is heard. For a repeating sound it is the repetition rate \(f_0 = 1/T\).
Harmonics
Sinusoids at \(f_0, 2f_0, 3f_0, \ldots\) that sum to a voice. They set timbre, not pitch.
Note · semitone · octave
A named pitch on the 12-tone scale (A4 = 440 Hz). Neighbours differ by a factor \(2^{1/12}\); 12 semitones double f.
Sinusoid · amplitude · phase
\(a\cos(2\pi f t + \varphi)\): its size a and its shift in time φ.
Time domain
The signal as its value at each instant: coordinates in the standard basis.
Frequency domain · spectrum
The signal as an amplitude and phase per frequency. The spectrum is the amplitudes against frequency.
Fourier transform
The invertible change of coordinates from the time domain to the frequency domain.
Frame · hop
A 40 ms slice of the recording (1,764 samples), taken every 10 ms.
Probe · inner product
A sampled sinusoid; its dot product with a frame measures how much of that frequency is present.
DFT · bin
\(X = Fx\) for N samples. \(X_k\) is bin k, at frequency \(k f_s/N\).
Orthogonal · unitary
Distinct rows of F have inner product 0. \(F^*F = N I\), so \(F/\sqrt N\) preserves length.
Periodic extension
The frame tiled end to end: the infinite signal the DFT implicitly analyses.
Aliasing
Frequencies k and k + N give identical samples, so they can't be told apart.
Circulant · convolution
A matrix whose rows are cyclic shifts of one row; multiplying by it is a cyclic convolution. F diagonalizes it.
DC · Nyquist · negative frequency
Bin 0 (zero frequency, N × the mean); bin N/2 = \(f_s/2\) for even N; bins above N/2 stand for k − N.
rfft
The DFT of a real input, returning only the \(\lfloor N/2\rfloor + 1\) bins that aren't mirror images.
Leakage · window · Hann
Energy spreading to other bins when the loop has a jump; a window fades the frame's ends; Hann is \(\tfrac12 - \tfrac12\cos\).
Decibel (dB)
\(20\log_{10}\) of an amplitude ratio: −20 dB is 10× smaller.
FFT · butterfly
Divide-and-conquer DFT in \(O(N\log N)\); a butterfly combines two half-size results.
Harmonic product spectrum
\(|X(f)|\,|X(2f)|\,|X(3f)|\): large only at the true \(f_0\).
Octave error
Reporting \(2f_0\) or \(f_0/2\) instead of \(f_0\).
Zero-padding
Appending zeros before the FFT: a finer frequency grid, no new information.
MIDI number · cent
\(m = 69 + 12\log_2(f/440)\), so a semitone is +1; a cent is 1/100 of a semitone.
Detune
The singer's constant offset from A440 tuning, in semitones, from −0.5 to 0.5.
Transposition · key
Shifting every note by the same amount: \(s - c\mathbf 1\).
Tolerance
The largest mean semitone error (0.5) that still counts as a match.
